I am an AI researcher working on embodied AI and the safety of physical AI systems. At the Singapore University of Technology and Design (SUTD), I build reinforcement learning agents (Soft Actor-Critic) that learn contact-rich control of a simulated musculoskeletal arm in MuJoCo, driven by intrinsic, information-theoretic rewards rather than hand-designed task rewards. The agents act through a low-dimensional muscle-synergy action space and learn forward models of body and environment dynamics from proprioceptive and tactile signals under partial observability.

As a Technical Alignment Research Accelerator (TARA) AI Safety Fellow, I am working on physical AI safety: a mechanistic study of how robot foundation models handle hazardous instructions, aimed at a lightweight runtime safety monitor. The fellowship also runs me through the ARENA curriculum in mechanistic interpretability, RL and RLHF, and LLM evaluations.

My path here ran through motor neuroscience and brain-machine interfaces at Rutgers, explainable AI under the DARPA XAI program (metrics for whether saliency explanations are perceptually and semantically faithful), and neurosymbolic representation learning at the University of Waterloo, where we encoded medical ontologies into transformers with vector-symbolic architectures (VSA). I then spent nearly three years building and deploying clinical machine learning at Canadian hospitals.

Musculoskeletal Reinforcement Learning with Intrinsic Rewards

Soft Actor-Critic agents that learn contact-rich control of a simulated musculoskeletal arm in MuJoCo, driven by intrinsic, information-theoretic rewards. The arm acts through a low-dimensional muscle-synergy action space, and the agents learn forward models of body and environment dynamics from proprioceptive and tactile signals under partial observability.
